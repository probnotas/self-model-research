What the run shows

  • The model was trained only on the healthy body and never retrained. Detection comes from prediction error alone.
  • No fault library, no labels, no extra sensor. The threshold is calibrated on healthy behaviour only, leave-one-out, so no run is scored against a threshold fitted to itself.
  • It does not depend on the controller working. Swing-up succeeded in 3 of 10 runs; all 10 detections fired anyway.

What it does not show

  • Detection, not diagnosis. It signals that the body changed, not which parameter moved.
  • Not a control result. This planner swings the pendulum upright in a minority of runs, and the damage left control quality unchanged (2.80 → 2.81).
  • Simulation, one body, one damage type. Recovering after damage is still unsolved in our own data.

One result worth arguing about

Probing the body with random torque produces a far bigger error jump — 9.0× against 3.8× under control — and yet it is the worse detector. At a zero-false-alarm threshold, random probing catches 1 of 10 damage events; closed-loop control catches 10 of 10. Thrashing the body widens the healthy baseline faster than it widens the damage signal. Holding the body inside a narrow, well-modelled regime is what makes the change legible at all.
