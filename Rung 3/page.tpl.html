Rung 3 · 7 conditions × 15 runs · recorded outcome

Nothing beat plain fine-tuning.

Rung 3 threw the two standard answers at damage recovery: an ensemble of five models with uncertainty-aware planning, and GrBAL-style meta-learning trained across a distribution of bodies. Both are what the literature reaches for. On the damage they were built for, neither separated from simply retraining one model on 100 samples.

Every condition, on the damaged body

Lower is better. The error bars overlap everything: the best model-based condition is standard errors from the do-nothing baseline, which is not a result. The model-free policy carried over from Rung 2 sits well below all of them.

The uncertainty penalty

Penalising ensemble disagreement helps by about 0.05 at its best setting — far inside the noise — and costs +2.5 to +2.7 when turned up. The knob is real, but what it buys at its best is not measurable here.

The one thing that did work

Meta-learning earned its keep only on damage it had never seen in training. On a 50% length change, meta-learning with the uncertainty penalty beat naive fine-tuning — the single comparison in all of Rung 3 that clears the noise floor.

There is a hint that naive fine-tuning on 100 samples is actively worse than not adapting at all on unfamiliar damage (z = +1.93), which would make sense — it overfits to a body it has barely met. At 15 runs that stays suggestive, not established.

Why the rung stalled

The blocker was not the methods, it was the measurement. Identical configurations moved by 1.3 between the validation and test seed sets — larger than every effect Rung 3 was trying to measure. With 15 evaluation episodes the noise floor sits above the signal, so most of these conditions are untestable rather than equal.

The fix is unglamorous and needs no new code: roughly 60 evaluation episodes, about 4× the compute. Rung 4 was built at 20 runs per condition with effects an order of magnitude larger than their spread, which is why its numbers resolve.

What it is worth taking forward

  • A negative result on a real question. Ensembles and meta-learning are the obvious answers to "adapt to a changed body", and on the damage they were trained for they bought nothing over one retrained model. That is worth knowing before building on them.
  • Meta-learning pays off out of distribution, not in it. The only significant effect showed up on damage never seen in training. If meta-learning has a role here, that is where to look for it.
  • Design the measurement before the method. Three conditions were built and evaluated before anyone checked whether the noise floor permitted an answer. Rung 4 reversed that order and the results came out legible.
