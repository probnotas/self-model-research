Rung 4 · 7 conditions × 20 runs

Did my body change, or did the world push me?

At the instant it happens, both look the same: the self-model's prediction stops matching reality. The question is whether the robot can tell them apart from the error alone — without being told, and without a second sensor. It can, in a second and a half, from a rule with no learned parts.

Prediction error after the event — same spike, different tail

The control that makes this real. The push is sized so its error spike matches or beats the body change's. The strongest case is the dashed purple line: it peaks higher than any body change and still comes straight back down. Peak size carries no information at all. Only persistence does.

The rule

Step 1 — detect
Error crosses the line
A 10-step trailing mean of prediction error passes a threshold set from undisturbed runs only, leave-one-out. No event data is used to place it.
Step 2 — wait
1.5 seconds
Long enough for a shove to have passed. Wait only 0.5 s and accuracy collapses to 39% — the world event has not finished yet.
Step 3 — decide
Still elevated?
Yes → body
No → world

Every condition

A harder limit than measurement

The pendulum's angular acceleration is:

thdd = 3g/(2l)·sin(th) + 3/(m·l²)·(u × torque_scale)

Mass and actuator gain enter only through the ratio torque_scale / m. Doubling the mass and halving the motor are not merely hard to tell apart — they are bit-identical, 0.000e+00 deviation over 500 shared commands. No forward model, no quantity of data and no better algorithm separates I got heavier from my motor got weaker on this body. They are one event wearing two names.

So the ladder has three steps, not two

01That something changed solved — 0.13 s (Rung 2)
02Whether it was my body or the world solved — 98% in 1.5 s
03Which parameter actually moved partly impossible in principle

What did not work

Directionality is not a signature. The hypothesis was that a body change would push the error consistently one way. It does not. Residual coherence later in the episode:

    A mass change decoheres, a length change coheres hard. Body changes share no common direction, so the whole idea has to be dropped. Persistence carries the signal alone.

    A gentle body change is invisible. Added damping produces a peak of 5.28e-05 against a 5.48e-05 baseline — underneath the detection floor. It fires in 10% of runs and is misclassified in both. That is a limit on detection, not on the rule, and it sets the sensitivity floor for the whole approach.
